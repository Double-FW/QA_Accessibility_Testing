# Persona-led customer experience reporting

Version 1.0 · 16 November 2026 · Derived practitioner guidance

Use functional personas to explain how the same service performs for people with different access needs. Personas describe tasks, access requirements and circumstances; they do not diagnose customers or stand in for research with disabled people.

## Build the report

1. Select the relevant [functional profiles](Functional_Profiles_and_Customer_Reach.md) and combinations for real service tasks.
2. Link each finding to affected profiles, source tests and observed or anticipated impact.
3. Estimate customer reach using compatible evidence, explicitly retaining unknowns and overlap.
4. Compare measured experience using [rolling research and A/B testing](Rolling_Research_and_AB_Testing.md).
5. Route barriers to the [managed backlog](../Reporting/Prioritisation_Tickets_and_Monthly_Reporting.md) and review monthly.

The EAA Annex I Section VII criteria provide the organising lens. This broader research use is an adaptation. Their legal use has specific conditions: they address functions not covered by preceding requirements; alternative use for specific technical requirements is conditioned on the Directive's terms and equivalent or increased accessibility. They do not replace applicable technical requirements.

Privacy is a requirement across accessible modes, not an eleventh demographic group. People can match multiple other profiles. Build intersectional scenarios, such as limited hearing plus limited vision, rather than forcing everyone into one exclusive persona.

## Minimum persona report

| Element | What to report |
| --- | --- |
| Persona definition | Local ID, functional criteria, task goal, chosen access methods, context and variation |
| Condition associations | Illustrative groups that may share the need, with evidence/limits; no presumed diagnosis |
| Reach | Customer denominator, measured/estimated/unknown count and percentage, source, assumptions, overlap |
| Test findings | Actual observations, affected journeys, test/execution/evidence IDs, status and confidence |
| Experience | Completion, effort, satisfaction, privacy and support; actual data or explicitly no data |
| Comparison | Same tasks/builds/instrument/period; comparator, gaps, sample and uncertainty |
| Action | Repair/research recommendation, owner, ticket, target date and verification |

A modelled persona walkthrough may identify candidate barriers, but cannot produce a genuine satisfaction score. Do not ask an LLM to impersonate a customer and label its ratings as customer evidence.

## Regulatory use

Customer evidence can substantiate claims about task access, effective adjustments, privacy, effort and service outcomes. It may strengthen an EAA, Equality Act or Section 508 evidence pack alongside applicable clause-level and process evidence. High satisfaction does not cancel an observed barrier or prove legal compliance. Neither a universal satisfaction threshold nor a universal equivalence margin is set by this reporting method.

Use [the legal boundaries](../Reporting/Sources_and_Legal_Boundaries.md). Keep technical conformance, functional outcomes, equality/adjustment assessment and satisfaction separate. A finding of “no statistically significant difference” is not proof of equivalent experience.
