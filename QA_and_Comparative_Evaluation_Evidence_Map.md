# Connecting technical QA and comparative work evaluation

This is an editorial application of the two resources. It connects questions and evidence without merging their methods, authority or result vocabularies. The QA framework organises tests and evidence; comparative task analysis examines actual work goals and system burden. Each adds information the other does not establish alone.

## Route the question to the appropriate method

| Question | Lead source | Evidence needed | Important limit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Which user outcome should be tested? | QA Canonical Test Library | Actual journey/component/state and applicable canonical outcome | Test selection is not execution |
| What relationship to WCAG is supported? | QA canonical WCAG relationship register | Exact relationship, applicability, full criterion conditions and scoped evidence | A mapping or atomic pass is not complete criterion conformance |
| What can automation establish? | QA framework and automated/manual cross-reference | Actual tool/version/configuration, rendered states and raw output | D/P/N or AXE mappings are not test results |
| Does a pattern work through keyboard or AT? | QA ACT-style and detailed mobile/web libraries | Reproducible procedure, environment, transitions and observation | Component success does not establish a complete work goal |
| What work is actually required? | Task-analysis Stage 1 and Stage 2 HTA | Goals, tasks, alternatives, conditions, handoffs and evidence provenance | A modelled ideal route is not actual work evidence |
| How do routes compare in time and demands? | Task methodology plus assessment safeguards | Matched outcomes, context, measured/reported data, assistance, errors and uncertainty | Mouse speed is a reference, not employee capability or universal acceptability |
| What burden accumulates over working time? | Task-analysis cumulative safeguards | Frequencies, non-overlapping time, blockers, effort, fatigue, privacy and opportunity | An extrapolation is not a measured loss or causal cost estimate |
| Did a repair restore useful access? | Both resources | Relevant technical retest plus complete-task/workflow evaluation | A closed issue or automated rerun does not prove restored independence |
| What should an accountable owner decide? | QA governance plus scoped task impact | Evidence, limitations, unresolved barriers, alternatives and actual delegation | Neither framework approves risk, exceptions or employment outcomes |

## A combined evaluation record

| Record element | What to preserve |
| --- | --- |
| Decision and scope | Product/system, work role or service, build, review purpose and intended outcomes |
| Work model | HTA goal, task/subtask IDs, execution plans, alternatives and mandatory dependencies |
| Context | Product/AT version, input, configuration, task data, environment, familiarity, training and assistance |
| QA references | Source library, exact ID/title, revision, local procedure and mapping role |
| Standards relationship | Owning register record, exact scope and relationship type, separately from repository policy |
| Actual execution | Local execution ID, procedure, steps, expected and observed behaviour, raw output/notes and evidence location |
| Task evidence | Direct observation, participant report, timing, errors, recovery, assistance, independence, effort, fatigue and privacy |
| Findings | Technical results, task outcomes, comparative burden and employment-impact inference as separate fields |
| Uncertainty | Not tested/blocked/cannot tell/tool error, missing perspectives, conflicting reports, sample and attribution limits |
| Remediation | System/process hypothesis, interim access, proposed permanent repair and receiving owner to confirm |
| Verification | Affected technical tests plus complete-workflow and cumulative retest, with actual results |
| Human decision | Scope, authorised owner, evidence and limits, unresolved questions, review trigger and decision actually made |

Use explicit links such as “supports”, “partially covers” or “observed during this task”. Do not overload an AXE rule, CTL entry, local procedure, run and HTA node into one identifier. A QA status and an employment-impact conclusion answer different questions and should not replace one another.

## A practical sequence

1. **Define the decision and work goal.** Identify required outcomes, actual organisational authority and voluntary participation/data boundaries. Consider known reports and existing technical findings immediately.
2. **Capture and model the task.** Preserve the task methodology’s capture-then-HTA structure. Include enabling tasks, errors, interruptions, support and alternative routes; mark unobserved elements honestly.
3. **Select technical evidence.** Use the QA source hierarchy for outcomes, standards relationships, detailed procedures and methods. Define states, environments and coverage limits.
4. **Evaluate in context.** Combine appropriate technical execution with task observation and participant accounts. Separate measurements, reports, inferences and unknowns. Avoid unnecessary exposure to known barriers in research.
5. **Analyse comparable outcomes and cumulative burden.** Keep success quality, independence, time, effort, recovery, privacy and opportunity distinct. Blocked work remains a blocker even when successful-task averages look acceptable.
6. **Repair the system and preserve interim access.** Use actual implementation evidence for root-cause claims. Prepare proportionate options and follow-up for owners rather than attributing the failure to the employee.
7. **Retest and report.** Recheck affected technical tests and full goals under stated comparable conditions. Explain changed versions and assumptions; report what evidence supports and what is still unresolved.

Participation by disabled people should inform framing and interpretation as well as evaluation. A technical scan does not replace that participation; a user study does not replace technical checks. Appropriate human safeguarding and authorisation are necessary before any live study or consequential action.

## Timing and cumulative calculations

For comparable completed tasks with a valid positive reference time, absolute delay is comparison time minus reference time; relative delay is that difference divided by reference time, multiplied by 100. State units, start/end events, reference condition and assistance. A zero or absent reference makes a relative percentage undefined; a blocked task does not have a successful duration of zero.

Estimate repeated additional time only from justified frequencies and non-overlapping components, with periods and uncertainty. Report what was measured separately from projected repetition or organisational cost. Do not add a recovery time already inside a task’s total, multiply uncertain success probabilities as if independent, or use successful-task averages to hide abandonment. Keep non-time burdens separate rather than force them into an invented common scale.

## Illustrative joined finding — not product evidence

In a fictional internal leave-request service, a keyboard route cannot operate the date control. A QA procedure could record the exact state, observed behaviour, expected operability and applicable source relationships. The HTA would show whether that control blocks the essential request goal and what assistance or alternative route is needed.

An assisted submission would not demonstrate independent completion. The evaluator would not assign the blocked route zero time, infer the worker lacks ability, or declare a legal conclusion from the example. A repair would need an appropriate technical retest and complete request-task verification, including confidentiality and practical effort. This scenario contains no measured result or claim about an actual employee.

## Reporting for action

Report the tested scope, actual result by method, task consequence, repeated burden where supported, uncertainty, interim protection, proposed repair and owner decision needed. Separate severity (user impact) from priority (which also reflects reach, frequency, criticality and dependencies). Use actual local rubrics when supplied and identify proposed judgement otherwise. Do not turn a pass ratio or timing threshold into an unsupported all-product accessibility claim.
